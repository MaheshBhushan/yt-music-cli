YOUR DATA & YOUR CHOICES

Privacy policy

ytm runs on your computer. Google sign-in connects the app to your account; it does not create an account on this website.

Effective 17 September 2026

Who is responsible

ytm is an independent, open-source YouTube Music terminal application maintained by Mahesh Koduri. For privacy questions or requests, contact mkoduri73@gmail.com. This policy covers the official ytm application and this website; modified versions may behave differently.

What the application accesses and why

  • Google authorization: when you choose Google sign-in, Google handles your password and consent. ytm receives OAuth access and refresh tokens so it can make authorized requests and keep your session working.
  • YouTube Music account data: ytm retrieves your library and playlists, playlist names and contents, track identifiers and metadata, and like information needed for the features you use. It sends your requested playlist changes and song likes to YouTube Music.
  • Search and playback: searches, track identifiers, requests for artwork and lyrics, and playback requests go to Google/YouTube and their content delivery services to provide the music interface and audio playback.
  • Optional browser import: only when you choose ytm auth --from-browser (or its remembered session is refreshed), ytm reads YouTube cookies from the selected local browser profile. Those cookies can authorize YouTube requests. Browser import is an alternative to OAuth, not a requirement for it.

The Desktop OAuth flow requests https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube. This is a broad YouTube account permission. ytm uses it for the account features described here, including reading your library and creating, editing, or deleting playlists and liking songs at your direction. It does not implement video uploading, channel deletion, or access to Gmail, Drive, or Google contacts.

Where data is stored

The official app does not send your OAuth tokens, library, or playlists to a developer-operated application server. It communicates directly from your device with the services needed for the features you run.

  • Credentials, remembered client configuration, browser-source settings, and any exported cookies are stored under ~/.config/ytm/. OAuth tokens are local files, not an encrypted credential vault. File permissions are restricted where supported; protect your device and backups.
  • Session state, remembered track metadata, local playlists, logs, and update-check information are stored under ~/.local/state/ytm/ by default.
  • If you explicitly use offline caching, downloaded audio is stored under ~/.cache/ytm/tracks/, with a default 2 GB cache limit and eviction of older unused tracks.
  • Some results and artwork are cached in memory while the app runs. Diagnostic logs can contain search text, track identifiers, actions, and error details. The TUI trace starts over on each launch; other local files remain until replaced, evicted, or deleted.

~ means your user home folder, including on Windows. XDG environment variables and YTM_TUI_LOG can change state, cache, or log locations. No automatic deletion period applies to all local files.

Sharing and other services

Google/YouTube receive the data needed to authenticate, retrieve account content, and carry out your actions, under Google’s Privacy Policy. mpv and yt-dlp run locally to resolve and play media; Google/YouTube and their media servers receive associated network requests. If enabled, a proof-of-origin token provider receives requests at your configured address (the default is local to your computer). A remote provider you configure can receive those requests under its own policies.

The app checks PyPI for releases when update checks are enabled. Installing or updating also contacts your configured package repositories. These services receive normal network information such as your IP address. ytm does not automatically upload its local diagnostic logs; information you deliberately include in an email or GitHub issue is received by those services and by the recipient. Public issues are public. Do not send passwords, tokens, cookie files, or unredacted credentials.

Limited Use of Google user data

ytm’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing music and account features described in this policy. It is not sold, used for advertising or credit decisions, or used to train general-purpose AI models. The maintainer does not receive automatic copies of your account data. If you voluntarily share specific diagnostic information for support, it is used to address that request, not for advertising or unrelated purposes.

Revoke access and delete local data

  1. Close ytm and stop playback.
  2. Open Google Account connections, select ytm, and remove its access. Revoking authorization stops future authorized access but does not delete files already on your computer.
  3. Delete the ytm folders under ~/.config/, ~/.local/state/, and ~/.cache/ to remove local credentials, preferences, session data, local playlists, logs, and cached audio. Check any custom locations you configured and your backups. This also deletes local playlists and settings you may want to keep.
  4. If you used browser-cookie import, remove local ytm credentials and do not run browser import again. For suspected cookie exposure, also sign out of or secure the relevant Google browser session.

Deleting local files or revoking access does not undo playlist edits or likes already saved in your Google account. Manage those separately in YouTube Music. The maintainer cannot remotely erase data from your device or Google account. For support correspondence you have sent to the maintainer, email a deletion request; retained copies may include email-provider backups or records required for legal or security purposes.

This website

This is a static information website hosted on Cloudflare. We do not add advertising, analytics scripts, tracking pixels, or a website sign-in form. Cloudflare processes technical request information, such as IP address, requested URL, and browser headers, to deliver and protect the site under its privacy policy. Hosting security and operational records are separate from the app’s local files. Email and GitHub support links take you to external services.
